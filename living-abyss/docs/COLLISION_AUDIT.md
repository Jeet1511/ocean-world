# Collision audit — current seven-destination build

## Scope and status
This is a partial technical audit, not a certification that every route is unobstructed. The current project is the selected seven-destination world; no world bounds, destinations or gate positions were changed in this pass. The pre-pass backup is `../../preservation/pre-collision-audit-pass.zip` (SHA-256 `610dcce46276ec42ff0ff1e3bd8a7fdb69b413368d72399256cc5a37033c5842`).

## Developer visualization
Press **`** (backquote) during gameplay to toggle collision diagnostics. Red shows solid analytic colliders and the actual upward-collision cavern roof meshes. Orange shows analytic collider AABBs and the world limit. Blue shows portal interaction trigger envelopes. Green shows the nearby rendered-triangle terrain grid and the swimmer collision radius, shared by dolphin and free-swim movement. The overlay is lazy-created, hidden in normal play, and does not participate in movement or raycasting. `window.ABYSS.collisionInfo()` exposes collider definitions and roof names for developer inspection. The green grid is local to the player, not a full-world navigation mesh.

## Findings and changes
1. **Rendered ground vs. collision mismatch.** The visible terrain cell is two triangles, but movement previously sampled a bilinear interpolation. Across 12,769 sampled cell centres, the mean absolute difference was 0.137 m, with a maximum 50.918 m at x = −18, z = −514. Collision now samples the actual triangles. Original bilinear samples remain for existing generation/placement and map logic. The large value also exposes an abrupt existing terrain height change; matching the rendered mesh does not smooth or remove that visible formation. Its underlying pad transitions need separate controlled investigation.
2. **Tower volumes.** Existing colliders were straight, stacked cylinders using a generic taper, ignoring the individual tower's generated lean and waist. Their conservative solid cores are now derived from that tower's actual generated rings and their individual bounds. This reduces overhang into empty water but can permit some edge penetration; it is not an exact mesh collider.
3. **Other active sources.** Controller collision uses world limits, terrain sampling, 779 active analytic rock volumes (306 cylinders, 12 capsules, 461 ellipsoids), and two actual roof meshes for upward movement. Seven portal volumes are non-solid triggers. Raycasting in `ABYSS.pick` is diagnostic, not a movement blocker. The roof test currently handles upward crossings, not all-direction cave wall collisions; other rock approximations remain approximate.
4. **Object provenance and broad-phase checks.** `collision_inventory.json` records every active analytic volume's ID, source, nearest destination, centre, AABB and source visual AABB. All 779 have source metadata. No exactly identical AABBs were found. These are not proof that each volume precisely matches its mesh; overlap between adjacent segments is often intentional, and the test does not detect all overlap or hidden geometry.
5. **AABB protrusion candidates** (over 2 m beyond the source formation's broad AABB; needs in-game inspection, not proof of a blocker):
   - `rock-collider-126`, wall:verdant, (360, −35.87, 290.22), 6.36 m.
   - `rock-collider-129`, wall:verdant, (443.57, −10.90, 240.56), 4.74 m.
   - `rock-collider-137`, wall:verdant, (680, −7.57, 269.78), 8.81 m.
   - `rock-collider-186`, arch:azurespan, (−585.01, −277.33, −302.04), 11.43 m.
   - `rock-collider-213`, wall:azurespan, (−560.49, −190.47, −379.33), 3.23 m.
   - `rock-collider-221`, wall:azurespan, (−310, −171.40, −350.22), 8.05 m.
   A full precise geometry correspondence, duplicate-overlap, arch-aperture, cave-opening, corridor and stale-geometry audit remains outstanding.

## Subsequent discovery — invisible rendered-geometry bug

A more detailed investigation of the user's screenshot location (approximately x −40, y −7, z 684) found that `makeBoulder()` produces **non-indexed** Three.js geometry. `mergeGeos()` copied its vertex attributes but only wrote indices for already-indexed geometry. Consequently, all merged reef-bommie boulder vertices were unreferenced and invisible, while their analytic ellipsoid colliders remained active. The first audit's AABB/provenance checks could not detect this: assigning a source and checking a source bounding box did not prove it was rendered. In particular `boulder:glasshorizon:19`, collider `rock-collider-337`, is close to the reported position.

`mergeGeos()` now creates sequential indices for non-indexed input. Existing boulders are rendered rather than removing their collision or geological features. `tests/geology-merge-regression.mjs` confirms no unreferenced vertices in the five merged geology meshes and confirms visible geometry is raycastable near the reported obstacle. Before/after screenshots at the same diagnostic pose are `../../preservation/visual-audit/before-boulder-merge.png` and `../../preservation/visual-audit/after-boulder-merge.png`. The later image reveals large overlapping, roughly modeled rock surfaces: the obstruction is no longer invisible there, but its visual quality and local clearance still require work. The screenshots do not establish that every reported blocker has been fixed.

A simulated 10-second swim from (−40, −7, 684) at fixed depth reached about 17 m north, 40 m south, 43 m east, and 6 m west before collision or obstruction in the straight-line probes. These are not route-completion tests: some directions encounter now-visible rock or terrain and were not individually verified from every approach. The collision cores are still approximate and some six earlier AABB candidates remain. More physical route testing, accurate geometry-fit inspection, and user-provided exact blocked locations/headings remain necessary.

## 2026-10-30: contact trace and screenshot positions
A new opt-in, bounded trace records actual collision responses without changing collision physics: in the browser console call `ABYSS.traceCollisions(true)`, reproduce the problem, then run `copy(JSON.stringify(ABYSS.collisionTrace(), null, 2))`. The returned last 200 contacts include swimmer position, yaw, pitch, keys, attempted position, and the actual solid's source, dimensions, debug ID and visual bounds where available; terrain, map boundary, roof and legacy-ceiling contacts are identified separately. `ABYSS.traceCollisions(false, false)` stops recording while retaining the trace. Backquote still toggles the developer overlay. Trace-disabled versus trace-enabled simulated motion was identical in regression testing. Browser smoke test enabled the trace, positioned the diagnostic swimmer within boulder:glasshorizon:0, and observed that source and bounds in the output without browser page errors. This is diagnostic positioning, not proof of navigation clearance.

Two reported positions (10,-6,606) and (-371,-15,228) were each simulated for ten seconds in four cardinal horizontal directions. Net horizontal displacement was 77.4–82.5 m at the former, 68.2–83.2 m at the latter; several boulders were contacted and slid along. These simulations do not establish the user's heading, reproduce the exact blockage, validate collider/triangle agreement everywhere, or constitute whole-world route coverage. Ask for a fresh-page reproduction, heading and trace before drawing conclusions. Previously revealed boulders are rough and may legitimately impede travel; further route and visual QA is required.

## Reported Glass Horizon obstruction at (46, -6, 640)
An automated 72-heading/pitch controller sweep reproduced near-sticking: at yaw 0 and pitch -0.35, six seconds of forward thrust moved only 1.97 m, with contacts from terrain and `boulder:glasshorizon:61`. Root cause confirmed for the seabed component: on every frame that downward-facing movement penetrated terrain, the old code damped the entire velocity to 55% and refused even gentle uphill motion once at clearance. It could present as an invisible barrier when looking slightly down. The controller now projects forward motion onto gentle ground, retains horizontal momentum, and continues to block/slide at steep terrain (rise limit 0.8 m per horizontal metre). The same diagnostic trajectory now travels 35.05 m; it continues to contact actual rocks. `tests/reported-wall-regression.mjs` reproduces the exact simulated setup, and `navigation-regression.mjs` checks a gentle slope and a sheer cliff. This is a physics simulation, not a verified recreation of the user's exact orientation. The 28 short gate approaches and geology merge checks pass. A headless keyboard-playback attempt timed out before reporting, so real-time browser movement is not verified for this change. Screenshots from the current build at this point show large rocks in at least some directions; no conclusion about the user's loaded version or other walls can be drawn. Remaining collider-to-mesh agreement, other obstructions, and full routes require investigation.

## 2026-10-31 — boulder mesh/core agreement
A directional triangle-ray audit over the 474 generated boulders (before gate aperture filtering) found 354 with the old ellipsoid boundary more than 1 m outside a sampled rock surface; 433 exceeded 0.1 m. At the reported Glass Horizon area, `boulder:glasshorizon:18` exceeded its ray-tested visible surface by approximately 5.55 m, and `boulder:glasshorizon:14` by 2.96 m. This independently confirms the user's report that sizable invisible collision volumes existed; the earlier bounding-box-only tests were insufficient.

Every boulder core is now fitted against all actual transformed boulder triangles in ellipsoid-normalised coordinates. The core is centred on the boulder generation centre, with a 2% interior margin. All 474 remain solid cores; after fitting, the same 16 horizontal ray directions per boulder found zero outward protrusions over 0.1 m. `tests/boulder-fit-regression.mjs` tests 12 irregular boulders against every triangle. The controller still adds the swimmer radius. Trade-off: because these are inscribed cores, players can penetrate some visible irregular ledges; they are not exact triangle collisions. The audit measures the raw core, not every possible real-world swimmer path. Other object families, cave/terrain seams, route coverage and actual user reproduction remain open and are not proven fixed.

Regression: reported screenshot-area six-second downward swim covers 39.24 m; 28 short gate approaches and terrain/cliff, trace and geology merge tests pass. No geometry, gate, control, destination, or entire collision system was removed.

## 2026-10-31: additional real wall and arch protrusions
User's new screenshot reads approximately X 2, Z 712, depth 7 m. A 72-heading/pitch synthetic sweep from (2,-7,712) moved 25.3–more than 40 m in the worst sampled horizontal directions over six seconds; this did not reproduce their unknown heading and does not dismiss the report. A broader actual-geometry comparison uncovered further phantom collision: of 24 authored curved-wall cylinders, 11 sampled more than 1 m beyond their own rendered wall at their mid-height; 4 of 12 arch capsule midpoints also exceeded the sampled horizontal mesh silhouette by >1 m. Worst sampled midpoint discrepancies were 16.80 m for `wall:verdant` at (680,-7.57,269.78) and 14.71 m for `arch:azurespan` at (-406.84,-161.22,-374.93). `wall_arch_collider_audit_pre_fix.json` records sampled object identities, coordinates, bounds and rays; this is not a full 3D exhaustive audit. Sampled 282 tower-cylinder midpoints had no >1 m horizontal protrusion, which is not proof of all tower surfaces.

Authored wall/arch analytic cylinders/capsules remain as broad-phase filters. Their narrow-phase now resolves against the actual baked wall/arch triangles, using sphere-to-triangle distance and ray-parity for a point already inside a closed shell. Empty water inside an oversized broad-phase volume is no longer pushed outward; actual contacted surfaces remain solid within broad-phase coverage. This does not prove perfect watertight meshes, wall coverage outside the old broad phase, or every collider type. The boulder conservative cores and terrain/roof collision remain unchanged. `tests/mesh-contact-regression.mjs` covers synthetic empty space, real surface and inside recovery; `tests/authored-wall-fit-regression.mjs` exercises an actual Verdant wall with both clear and blocked points (32 and 40 of 72 sampled). In Chromium the 24 wall and 12 arch guards were present, the same wall samples reported 32 clear and 40 contacts, and eight rendered frames of W at the screenshot position moved from (2,-7,712) to (2,-7.19,711.06); no page or non-favicon console errors. This is a short smoke test, not the user's exact reproduction or a whole-world route test.

Also corrected the HUD's misleading instruction: Q is dive and E is rise; A/D turn while Z/C strafe. No key bindings changed. A local Node microbenchmark reported ~0.033 ms per wall guard call for 700 triangles over 5,000 calls on this sandbox; this is not a target-hardware frame-rate result.

## 2026-10-31: image-7 report near (58, -3, 724)
The user reports an invisible wall in this vicinity. The exact heading and path are not shown, so this remains unconfirmed rather than dismissed. A six-second controller sweep of 24 headings and three pitches from approximately (58,-3,724) showed a minimum net horizontal displacement of 30.58 m, with terrain and `boulder:glasshorizon:64` contacts on that sample. A real Chromium keyboard test at the same diagnostic pose, yaw 0/pitch 0, moved north 5.93 m in four rendered frames with no recorded contacts; software rendering was exceptionally slow. Neither test recreates the user's unknown heading or proves that the surrounding area or entire world has no invisible wall. Ask for the movement direction and contact trace/overlay at the actual stuck point if it persists.

A separate timing defect could make controls feel nearly stuck during poor rendering performance: frame delta was capped to 0.05 s, causing time and movement to slow down dramatically below 20 FPS. This is now capped at 0.25 s, with movement subdivided into steps of at most 0.05 s so collision stepping remains bounded. Long stalls beyond 0.25 s still deliberately slow simulation rather than jumping or tunnelling; no hardware performance claim is made. Input keys are cleared on iframe/window blur to prevent stuck controls after lost keyup events. This timing repair is not proof the reported collision is solved.

## 2026-10-31: portal clearance, instanced rock render and surface collision
Gate site selection now checks rendered geology bounds, retained analytic volumes and local terrain; no collision volumes are removed near portals. `tests/portal-geometry-surface-regression.mjs` checks all seven sites (zero registered geology AABB overlaps within a 12.5 m horizontal by 20 m vertical envelope), 28 short cardinal approaches with collision enabled, and triangle contact on 12 visible Glass Horizon boulder samples. Existing approach/reported-wall tests no longer simulate the obsolete gate-area collider deletion. One previously obstructing Glass Horizon boulder was moved 25 m in X, retained as solid, and the reported simulation moved 40.48 m. Seven browser portal-dialog clicks passed; diagnostic positioning and local tests do not prove continuous-world routes.

Triangle guards, created on demand and cached for 24 recent objects, now augment collision coverage for 1,234 authored geology and large instanced scree surfaces. Analytic collision remains; small decorative rubble remains non-solid. Separately, the rock material vertex shader was found to ignore `instanceMatrix`: instanced rocks could be drawn in the wrong position while collision referred to their intended position. That is fixed and was checked by actual WebGL pixel readback (box drawn at x=3, origin empty). The prior conservative core fitting remains relevant to boulders but is now supplemented by triangle guards. Guard approximation, non-watertight surfaces, high-speed tunnelling, cave visual meshes, all locations and target-hardware performance still require physical investigation.
